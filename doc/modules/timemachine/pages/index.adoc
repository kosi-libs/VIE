= TimeMachine

TimeMachine is a live inspector for VIE ViewModels.
A _probe_ in your application streams every Model, Intent and Signal of the ViewModels it observes to the _inspector_, a desktop app that shows them as a timeline per ViewModel.
The inspector can also send an object back into a live ViewModel, to drive it into a state that is hard to reach by hand.

IMPORTANT: TimeMachine is not published yet.
Its modules build from a checkout of VIE, where the task-list demo in `demo/` has the probe wired in.


[#scope]
== What it does

TimeMachine observes and intervenes.
It does not rewind, restore snapshots or persist anything: the inspector keeps what it hears in memory, and starts empty again when it restarts.

A session is one run of your application.
The inspector lists the sessions it has heard from, the ViewModels of each, and for each ViewModel the Models, Intents and Signals it went through, in order.


[#modules]
== Modules

[cols="2,1,4"]
|===
| Module | Role | Holds

| `vie-timemachine-client`
| Probe
| The library your application embeds, for every platform VIE supports.
It observes one ViewModel per middleware, keeps one WebSocket per application process, and applies what the inspector sends.

| `timemachine-server`
| Inspector
| A Compose Desktop application.
It runs the WebSocket server, keeps the sessions, ViewModels and timelines in memory, and shows them.

| `vie-timemachine-shared`
| Contract
| The wire messages, the JSON configuration, the connection coordinates and the protocol version, which both other modules depend on.
|===

The module names follow the WebSocket roles: your application is the WebSocket client, and the inspector is its server.
The pages say probe and inspector.


[#vocabulary]
== Vocabulary

Each of these words names one thing, in these pages and in the code alike.

Probe:: What runs in your application between `TimeMachine.start` and `TimeMachine.stop`: it observes the ViewModels and talks to the inspector.
Inspector:: The desktop application the probe connects to, which shows what it receives.
Client and server:: The two ends of the WebSocket, and nothing more.
They name the modules and the two directions of the protocol, whose messages are `TimeMachineClientMessage` and `TimeMachineServerMessage`: the probe is the client, and the inspector is the server.
Session:: One run of your application, as the inspector lists it.
Kind:: Which of the three an object is: a Model, an Intent or a Signal.
Type:: The class of a ViewModel's objects of one kind, such as `CounterVIE.Intent`, with its subtypes when it is sealed.
The probe describes it to the inspector as a `VIETypeInfo`.
Observation:: One Model, Intent or Signal that the probe saw go through a ViewModel, as it reports it.
Intervention:: What the inspector asks of the probe, and what the probe answers: an object to send into a live ViewModel.
Injection:: What the probe does to carry out an intervention: it puts the object into the ViewModel, through the ViewModel's own public API.
The answer to an intervention says that the probe accepted it, not that the injected object arrived (see xref:protocol.adoc#intervention[Intervention]).

The names in the code follow one more rule.
A name that starts with `VIE` describes something of the application the probe observes, such as `VIEObservation`, `VIEViewModelInfo`, `VIETypeInfo` or `VIEObjectKind`.
A name that starts with `TimeMachine` is a part of the tool itself, such as `TimeMachineConfig`, `TimeMachineSession` or `TimeMachineClientMessage`.
